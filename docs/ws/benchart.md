# Workstream `benchart`: models and textures for the three new workbenches

Branch `benchart`. Art only: no blockstates, loot tables, lang or Java. All geometry and pixels are generated by the
scripts in `tools/benches/`. Nothing is copied from the old workbench models or textures.

## Files delivered (under `patch/assets/frontierhunts/`)

|---|---|---|---|
| `frontier_workbench` | `models/block/frontier_workbench_{left,right,inventory}.json` | `models/item/frontier_workbench.json` | `textures/block/bench/frontier_workbench_atlas.png` (256²), `..._wood.png` (32², particle) |
| `gunsmith_bench` | `models/block/gunsmith_bench_{left,right,inventory}.json` | `models/item/gunsmith_bench.json` | `textures/block/bench/gunsmith_bench_atlas.png` (256²), `..._wood.png` |
| `reloading_bench` | `models/block/reloading_bench_{left,right,inventory}.json` | `models/item/reloading_bench.json` | `textures/block/bench/reloading_bench_atlas.png` (256²), `..._wood.png` |

GUI emblems (64×64, transparent, dark 1 px rim + soft shadow, readable at 32 px):
`textures/gui/bench/{frontier_workbench,gunsmith_bench,reloading_bench}_emblem.png`. The frontier one is a handsaw
crossed with a claw hammer. The gunsmith one is a recurve bow behind a rifle scope. The reloading one is a red
O-frame press with a loaded cartridge.

Every model sets `"ambientocclusion": true`, `textures.atlas` and `textures.particle`
(`frontierhunts:block/bench/<id>_wood`). There is no `render_type`, so all faces are opaque (solid layer).

## Orientation (verified)
- `WideStationBlock.getStateForPlacement` sets `facing = player.getHorizontalDirection().getOpposite()`. So the
  facing points toward the player, and in model space (facing=north) the front is the north face, z = 0.
- `WideStationBlock.partner()` puts the RIGHT part at `facing.getClockWise()` (north → east = +x). `Kit.wide()` (the
  living-world camps) does the same. So the left half is x 0..16 and the right half is x 16..32, shifted by -16.
- The jar's `blockstates/weapons_workbench.json` maps `facing=north/east/south/west` to `y = 0/90/180/270` for
  `bench_part=left/right`. The new models follow the same convention. The old models use a custom
  `frontierhunts:field_shelter` loader, so their geometry was not used for this check: the Java above is the
  authority.
- Seen by a player standing at the front, the x = 32 end is on their **left**. That is where the frontier leg vise,
  the shotshell press and the gunsmith rod stand sit.

## Geometry contract compliance (`tools/benches/check_models.py` passes with 0 problems)
- Work surface at y = 15 (laminated top y 12..15). The back wall stands at z 14.5..16, with its cap rail up to
  y = 26.
- Every element in each half lies within x/z 0..16 and y 0..26. Rotations use one axis and ±22.5/45 only. No rotated
  element straddles the split, and UVs stay within 0..16.
- No coplanar overlapping same-facing faces (z-fighting), checked across the joined halves.
- Hidden faces are culled by coverage sampling. The cut faces at the x = 16 split are removed too. Faces lying on a
  block boundary below y = 16 carry `cullface`, and bottoms on the ground carry `cullface: down`.

| model | left | right | inventory | authored parts | culled faces |

| gunsmith_bench | 93 | 81 | 174 | 151 | 324 |

Each half is under the ~180 element budget. The inventory model is just left plus right joined, so it can exceed the
budget; it is only used for the item.

## How the textures are made
- Each bench has one baked 256² atlas at 32 px per block (2 texels per model pixel). Every visible face gets its own
  texel region with a 1 px bleed gutter.
- Each texel is shaded from a **3D solid procedural material** at its model-space position:
  - Wood is rings around an offset pith with fbm warp, latewood bands, pores and streaks, and darker end grain. Grain
    is continuous across faces and across the split.
  - Metals: mottled and brushed iron/steel, blued steel, brass ramp, chrome env-banding, and painted cast iron with
    paint chips.
  - Felt, leather, rope, canvas and glass (with contents, such as powder in the hopper and shot in the bottle).
- Painted details:
  - knife scratches, dents and cup rings on the tops
  - drawbore pegs on the legs and breadboard ends
  - saw teeth and etch
  - stitched leather
  - ammo-box labels, stencilled ammo cans, flare bands and powder-can labels
  - scale beam ticks, lens glints, parts in the trays, and the lamp mouth glow
- Lighting baked in, all view-independent so it is correct for every facing:
  - ray-traced **ambient occlusion** against the bench itself and the ground (36 rays per texel)
  - worn highlights on exposed edges and darkened seams where boards meet flush
  - a mild sky gradient on vertical faces
  - fake round shading on bars and cylinders, with a gloss streak on polished parts
- Cylinders are a core box plus two side strips: a chamfered octagon-like section with no overlapping caps.

## Design notes
- **Family:**
  - Every bench uses the same heavy frame (`common.py`): 2.5 px legs with drawbore pegs, end aprons and low
    stretchers, a three-plank laminated top with breadboard ends, a slatted shelf, and a framed board-and-batten back
    wall with a cap rail.
  - All three have the same 15 px work height and the same proportions.
- **Frontier Workbench**:
  - Timber: warm golden oak.
  - Under the top: a leg vise (dark oak chop, turned rosewood screw hub, tommy bar, parallel guide), three darker
    drawers with turned knobs, and a shelf with folded canvas, a wool blanket, a crate of tent pegs and a strapped
    hide bedroll.
  - On the top: a black-japanned No.5 jack plane with shavings, stitched leather panels with a head knife, an awl and
    a red thread spool, and a rotated carver's mallet.
  - Tool wall (old greyed-oak boards, peg rail): handsaw, claw hammer, chisel rack, coiled rope, leather strips, and
    a tin lantern with a warm glass.
- **Gunsmith's Bench**:
  - Timber: dark figured walnut.
  - On the top: a green felt mat with a border and wear, and a padded black gun cradle (oxblood leather pads)
    holding a walnut and blued-steel bolt rifle.
  - Also on the top: a scope lying on the mat, two angled parts trays, a driver block, a cleaning-rod stand, and a
    brass banker's lamp with a green shade.
  - Under the top: a bank of six small brass-pulled parts drawers with card labels, a long tool drawer, a hard rifle
    case and an oil can.
  - Back wall: raised panels with a recurve bow on brass pegs.
- **Reloading Bench**:
  - Timber: pale honey pine.
  - On the top:
    - a red cast-iron O-frame press with a chrome ram, a case in the shell holder, a die and lock ring, and a lever
      with a ball knob
    - a powder measure on a red stand with a glass hopper half full of powder
    - a green beam scale with a brass pan
    - two loading blocks of brass, with bullets seated in one row, and loose rounds
    - a blue shotshell press with red hulls and shot and powder bottles
    - a fletching jig holding a vertical arrow with yellow, red and white vanes, and two finished arrows with
      broadheads
    - a red flare canister
  - Back wall: two shelves of colour-coded ammo boxes, powder cans and primer trays.
  - Under the top: two OD ammo cans and a crate of shotshell boxes.
- **Distinctness:** golden oak with a tool wall, dark walnut with green felt, a bow and a lamp, and pale pine
  crowded with red, blue and brass. They read apart at 8+ blocks (`previews/lineup.png`) and at 32 px.
- **Item transforms** are fitted numerically (`build_benches.py:item_model`). The gui view is `[28,150,0]`, which
  shows the front and the x = 0 end, scaled so the 2-wide bench fills the slot.

## Tools
- `tools/benches/build_benches.py <repo> [ids]`: builds the models, atlases, particle tiles and item models, and
  writes `stats.json`.
- `tools/benches/preview_bench.py <repo> <id> out.png [--views front,fl,fr,back | yaw:pitch] [--gui]`: a software
  renderer for the two halves placed together on a block-grid ground, with vanilla face shading. `--gui` renders the
  item through its gui transform at 32/64/128 px.
- `tools/benches/check_models.py`: limits and z-fight checks. `emblems.py` paints the emblems. `lineup.py` renders
  the distance lineup.
- `make_previews.sh` regenerates `tools/benches/previews/*.png`.
