Branch `smalls` (on top of `fh2-gear20` 6f5ad6f). Previews and mocks: `docs/ws/smalls/*.png`.

## 1. Blood-Tracking Lamp: a real hand-held light

Austin: "the blood-tracking lamp is really bad quality when holding". The old held model was 23 crossed boxes (an
octagon faked with three rotated boxes per section) with 16 px textures, held almost upright in first person.

Now `tracking_lamp_3d` is a **neoforge:obj mesh** (1812 faces, smooth normals) generated by `tools/smalls/lamp_art.py`:
a modern blood-tracking light about 16 cm long - domed rubber tail switch in an anodized tailcap, **diamond-knurled
grip**, **olive rubber armour sleeve** with three moulded ribs, a moulded blood-drop logo and a **rubber side switch**
boot, the armour flaring into a **finned aluminium head**, a **bead-blasted steel strike bezel with eight
crenellations**, the **teal tracking filter** over an orange-peel reflector and the LED die, and a **blaze-orange
paracord wrist lanyard** through an eyelet under the tailcap, with a cord lock.
- Textures (original, procedural, `textures/item/tracking_lamp/{grip,anodized,armour,bezel,lens,rubber,cord}.png`,
  16-128 px each, one sprite per material).
- The lens material has `Ka 1 1 1` with `emissive_ambient`: the lens is full-bright (the lamp is on while held; blood
  shows red). Everything else is normally lit and shaded.
- Transforms (checked in the previews below): first person right/left (low right of the view, lens forward and turned in
  toward the crosshair, the whole light incl. lanyard visible, clear of crosshair and hotbar; Minecraft mirrors the left
  hand), third person (grip in the fist, lens where the player faces, lanyard under the wrist), ground (on its side),
  item frame (lying across), head.
- GUI: `separate_transforms` keeps a flat 32x32 icon, now rendered from the mesh (`tools/smalls/lamp_preview.py`,
  box-filtered, hard alpha, dark outline like the other gear icons).
- Function unchanged: `OutfitterItem.lamp(player)` (blood highlight in `TrailClient`) only checks the item id.
- `tools/art/gear_art.py` no longer writes the lamp icon (it would overwrite the new one); its old `lamp_model()` call
  is removed from `main()`.
- Previews: `lamp_firstperson_right.png`, `lamp_firstperson_left.png` (1920x1080, FOV 70, the real
  ItemInHandRenderer / ItemTransform math), `lamp_thirdperson.png` (ItemInHandLayer math on a Steve-sized figure),
  `lamp_ground.png`, `lamp_turntable.png`, `lamp_closeup_front.png`, `lamp_closeup_tail.png`, `lamp_icon_x8.png`.
  Re-render: `python3 tools/smalls/lamp_art.py <repo> && python3 tools/smalls/lamp_preview.py <repo> tracking_lamp_3d <out>`
  (the preview script also writes the icon into patch/).

## 2. Food tab audit ("is that all there is?")

Yes: the Frontier Workbench Food tab (pemmican, honey pemmican, raw game sausage) is **every food that is crafted**.
Everything else edible is cooked, dried, aged, harvested or processed in the crafting grid. Nothing food-related sits on
another bench tab, and every food has a source. Full list (every item with a food component in frontierhunts):

| food | how you get it |
|---|---|
| Raw Venison, Backstrap, Heart & Liver, Fat Trimmings, Raw Wild Game, Raw Bear Meat, Raw Wild Fowl, Venison Quarter | harvesting the animal (skinning tool / field dressing) |
| Raw Venison (more) | **crafting grid**: venison quarter (or aged quarter) + skinning tool / field knife (`butcher`) -> 4 (6) |
| Aged Venison Quarter | hang a venison quarter on the game pole |
| Roasted Venison, Seared Backstrap, Roast Wild Game, Roast Bear, Roast Wild Fowl, Seared Heart & Liver | furnace, smoker or campfire |
| Rendered Tallow | fat trimmings in a furnace, smoker or campfire |
| Jerky | raw meat strips on the drying rack (Camp tab) |
| **Pemmican** | **Frontier Workbench · Food**: 2 jerky + tallow + any berry -> 3 |
| **Honey Pemmican** | **Frontier Workbench · Food**: 2 jerky + tallow + honey bottle -> 3 |
| **Raw Game Sausage** | **Frontier Workbench · Food**: 2 raw wild game + fat or tallow + salt -> 4 |
| Smoked Game Sausage | raw game sausage in a smoker or on a campfire |
| Venison Stew, Venison Chili, Pot-Roasted Bear, Backstrap & Mushrooms, Wild Fowl with Berry Sauce, Wild Fowl & Grain Pilaf, Heart & Liver Fry, Hunter's Breakfast, Smoked Fish Chowder, Camp Bannock | the Camp Dutch Oven (`camp_cooking`) |
| Spoiled Meat | meat left to spoil |
| (any meat) salt-cured | **crafting grid**: meat + salt (`salt_cure`, keeps it fresh longer; same item) |

Salt (not a food) comes from a water bottle in a furnace / on a campfire. The two crafting-grid recipes (butcher,
salt cure) are special recipes that work on the stack you have (knife damage, freshness), so they stay in the grid.

Helpful touch: a recipe's paper card now shows a hint over its status line for some tabs (`BenchScreen.hintFor`):
Food: *"Dry jerky on a drying rack. Cook more at a campfire, smoker or Dutch oven."* (mock: `frontier_workbench_food.png`).

## 3. Arrowheads at the Reloading Bench

Austin did not find how to put a head on an arrow. Three changes make it obvious:

1. **Arrows tab shows arrows made with each head**, as craftable tiles: section *Hunting arrows*: Fixed-Blade Broadhead
   (the stock arrow), Field Point, Mechanical Broadhead, Cut-on-Contact Broadhead, Judo Point, Tracer Broadhead, Tracer
   Field Point, Glacier Tracer Broadhead; *Primitive arrows*: Knapped Flint Point (stock), Obsidian Point, Bone Point;
   *Bowfishing*. Each fitted one is a bench recipe (`patch/data/frontierhunts/recipe/arrows/*.json`, 9 files):
   **stick + feather (the shafts) + 4 heads -> 4 arrows carrying that head** (`custom_data {arrow_tip}`; its name is
   "Hunting Arrow · Field Point"). If you lack the heads but have their materials, the craft **makes the heads first**
   with the bench's head recipe (`BenchCrafting.withHeads`, whole batches, server-authoritative; the card says
   "Ready · 4 heads made from your materials" and the head line is gold instead of red). Craft x5 works too. Tiles name
   the head and carry a small head badge; the card hint says "Stick and feather make the shafts. Missing heads are made
   from your materials." (mock: `reloading_bench_arrows.png`).
2. The **"Refit arrows" tab is now "Fit arrowheads"**, with a one-line explanation under its title ("Puts a new head on
   arrows you have. The old heads come back to you."), buttons "Fit 1" / "Fit all (n)" (mock: `reloading_bench_refit.png`).
3. Tooltips: arrow tips say "Fit at the Reloading Bench: Fit arrowheads." / "Or make arrows with this head on its Arrows
   tab."; arrows say "Change heads at the Reloading Bench: Fit arrowheads."; Arrow tips cards hint the same.
- Handbook step 3 (`onboard.frontierhunts.step.3.p2`) explains both ways; its recipe row now shows field point,
  broadhead, **Hunting Arrow · Field Point** (the new recipe, "Reloading Bench · Arrows") and the fit card ("Reloading
  Bench · Fit arrowheads"). Task texts and the journal hint follow. Making a fitted arrow credits the TIPS task (the
  existing `fittedArrow` check) and the ARROWS task (crafted field_arrow).

## Risks / notes
- First OBJ **item** model in the mod (blocks already use neoforge:obj). Parent inheritance of the custom geometry
  (separate_transforms base -> `tracking_lamp_3d`) is NeoForge-standard (`BlockGeometryBakingContext.getCustomGeometry`
  walks the parent). Iris/Sodium draw item models through vanilla paths: fine; worth a glance with shaders.
- 1812 quads drawn for one held item: negligible; nothing per frame in code.
- The disk was full during this work (resv cap); the clone lived in /dev/shm/smalls (see report).
- If a later branch adds arrow tips, add `arrows/<shaft>_<tip>.json` (and a `mock_bench.py` TIP_TITLE entry).
